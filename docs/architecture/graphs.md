# Graphs

`ScrollableGraphWidget`, the single time-series graph class: fit, threshold steps, drawing cost, touch routing and fullscreen. Hard rules are summarized under **Invariants** in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md); this file holds how the subsystem works and why.

## ScrollableGraphWidget

- `ScrollableGraphWidget` (`app/ui/raw_eeg_screen.py`) is the single time-series graph class — every per-session/diary graph is an instance of it.
- **Plot area.** `_label_pads` sizes the margins from the labels at each draw: left the axis's widest label, right the lines' end values and the scalar threshold's label (drawn in that margin), bottom the time rows, and either side at least half a time label (a mark at the plot's edge centres its label on it). They only grow while the data lasts (`_pads_floor`, reset by `load_static_data` / `clear_data`), so an auto-scaled peak crossing a power of ten or a value gaining a digit doesn't shift the plot sideways at 2 Hz. `_plot_rect()` is the one plot rectangle: drawing, glyphs and the pan code read it. A line's end label takes the nearest free slot between the plot's bottom and the widget's top (`_free_label_y`, upward first), since the top margin leaves no room above and the bottom one holds the time rows; a threshold step's label at the top of the scale goes under its line. The Y grid is bounded: a fixed `grid_step` that would draw more than `_MAX_Y_GRID_LINES` lines gives way to `_compute_nice_step` (each line carries a label; a 200000 scale at the default 20-unit step was seconds per redraw).
- **Glyphs** sit inside the plot's corners (`_corner_rect`): the series picker top-left, expand top-right (in fullscreen the close cross, `set_expand_callback(cb, closes=True)`), fit bottom-right — fit hidden on a plot too short for it under expand. `AppManager._wire_graph_affordances` wires all three on every graph (`_all_graphs()`), the one place for cross-graph affordances. They are strokes in `TC.TEXT` straight on the graph, no backing (`_draw_icon_backing` only sets the colour), so they contrast on every palette; a fixed backing reads as a grey box on light themes.
- **Glyph touches.** A glyph is a button over the data: `on_touch_down` grabs a touch on it like any other and records the glyph in the finger's record (`_glyph_at`); while the finger stays on it and within `_GLYPH_DRIFT_DP` of where it landed (a drifting fingertip), it stays pressed (a light disc, `_pressed_glyph`) and fires on release where a clean tap would place a marker; more drift or leaving the glyph turns the touch into an ordinary gesture. A second finger makes the gesture a pinch or a two-finger tap, never a tap: no glyph fires and no marker is placed. The press is the lifted finger's own (`on_touch_up` reads the finger that lifted, not the one left down). So a pan, pinch or vertical swipe that starts on a glyph still works. The hit-test is in window coordinates (`_touch_in_window_rect`), so the ScrollView can't starve a glyph the way it would an overlaid Kivy widget.
- **Fit the whole session** (#43): `set_fitted(True)` shows all of the data, never in a window narrower than the graph's default (a session just started would be a second stretched across the plot; `_fit_restore` keeps the width and scroll position to go back to), and `_follow_fit` keeps it whole wherever the data's length changes — `add_point` / `add_points_batch` (a live session growing), `load_static_data` / `clear_data` (another session: going back then lands at its end). A tap fits the graph's zoom group together (`toggle_fit`, over its `_sync_group`). Raw EEG holds only its last minute, so fitted it shows that; a session longer than the 3 h buffer (`GRAPH_POINTS_MAX`) shows its last 3 h. Any `_set_viewport` — a pinch, the wheel, a linked graph's zoom — ends the fit; a zoom from a fit starts from the group's widest fitted span (`_zoom_base`), not the zoomed graph's own (raw EEG's is capped at its minute). A live session's reload after a screen lock is the same session (`load_static_data(..., new_session=False)`): the fit's way back and the margins carry on. A fitted long session redraws an order of magnitude slower than the default window (every sample folded), which is fine for the detail (it redraws on a touch) and the same as zooming a live graph out by hand.
- **Zoom groups.** `link_zoom` links graphs so a zoom on one zooms all: the requested window travels (each graph converts it through its own sample rate and clamps it to its own limits, so raw EEG's one-minute cap never narrows the others), and the group is aligned to the first graph's window as it is linked. There are two groups, the live graphs and the session detail's (`AppManager._link_graph_zoom` over `_live_graphs()` / `_detail_graphs()`): the saved zoom (`graph_zoom_seconds`) is the live metrics graph's `zoom_seconds` — its window before any fit — so a detail zoomed out over a long session must never reach it. Loading it (`AppManager._apply_saved_zoom`) starts both groups at it; only the live group's is ever saved. A fit is never propagated through the link. The window's mouse wheel reaches every graph; `_on_window_mouse_down` ignores one not attached to the window (`get_root_window()`), since a graph on a hidden screen or tab keeps its last geometry under the pointer.
- **Series picker.** The picker is wired on **every** graph (live metrics/raw/band + diary metrics/raw/freq) and gated to graphs with **>1 series**, since a single-series graph's picker could only blank the line. One presenter, `_present_series_picker(graph)`, reads the graph's **own** catalog/labels/colors (`series_keys()` / `series_name()` / `series_color()`) — no hardcoded metric list. Each graph carries a `graph_id` (`live_metrics`, `live_band`, `diary_metrics`, `diary_freq`, …) and its selection persists per-graph as `graph_series_<graph_id>` (JSON, per-user); the live metrics graph additionally migrates legacy `toggle_<key>` rows when it has no JSON yet, other graphs default to all-visible. `set_visible` fires the graph's `visibility_callback`, so the owning screen's legend (visible-filtered) and the fullscreen legend both refresh on any picker toggle *or* restore. The **custom_formula** validity gate (`self._custom_formula.is_valid`) applies only to the live metrics graph — diary custom_formula is recorded data and toggles freely.
- `ScrollableGraphWidget.set_threshold_steps(steps)` / `threshold_value_at(tick)` — piecewise threshold rendering: the dashed threshold line follows a list of `(start_tick, target)` steps instead of a single scalar. The live metrics graph and the session detail's step it from the ticks' targets (#83), simple and program sessions alike: live, `add_point(values, threshold=)` extends the steps with `extend_steps` at each point (the tick's target rides `_ui_update` and the mirror's `score_target`, so `_reload_live_graphs_from_mirror` rebuilds the same steps with `target_steps`; `_start_session_common` clears them, and a segment switch no longer sets the line); the detail's `detail_graph_data` returns `threshold_steps` (`target_steps` over the rows: None unless every row has a target) and the metrics fill applies them. A session from before #83 keeps what the open draws from its row: its program's steps (`sessions.session_program`, mapped through elapsed time) or one line at `threshold_used`. Single-value threshold path unchanged (`set_threshold(value)` still works; `steps=None` reverts to scalar).

## Drawing cost

- **Graph drawing cost** (`ScrollableGraphWidget._draw`, rebuilt from scratch on every `add_point` — 2 Hz per live graph — and re-rendered by GL every frame): (1) axis/value labels come from `_LABEL_CACHE` (LRU of 512 `CoreLabel`s keyed by text/size/colour; the `CoreLabel` is kept, not just its texture, because it is the texture's reload observer after an Android GL context loss) — a redraw used to render ~70 fresh text textures; (2) the X grid step is `_x_grid_step(graph_w, visible_seconds)`, the smallest of 10 s … 2 h that keeps time labels ≥70 dp apart (fixed 10 s drew ~160 overlapping labels at a 26-min zoom; default zooms keep 10 s), each label `_elapsed_label` — `M:SS` under an hour, `H:MM:SS` from one (`75:00` read as minutes); (3) a heatmap series (`set_heatmap_color`, the live shamatha line) is **one `Mesh`**: a quad per segment plus a square patch per vertex (Kivy `Line.width` is the half-thickness, so the patch half-size is `dp(line_width)`), each vertex's texture v = its height in the 1×64 blue→red `_heatmap_texture()` (clamp-to-edge, refilled by a reload observer), split at `_MESH_MAX_QUADS` = 65535 // 6 = 10922 quads, because Kivy caps a Mesh at 65535 *indices* (GLES2, enforced on Android; 6 per quad) — the first limit counted vertices (16000 quads), so zooming out over ~45+ min of a live session (>5461 visible points) raised `GraphicException` and crashed the app — it was a `Color`+`Line` per segment (~600 instructions per redraw and ~600 draw calls per frame). A stencil mask over a gradient was tried and rejected: Kivy counts overlapping mask fragments (wide-line joints), so `equal` leaves holes and `lequal` leaks outside an enclosing ScrollView's stencil — and `StencilUse(func_op=...)` in the constructor is silently ignored (only the property setter applies; the getter raises on Python 3); (4) `set_drawing_enabled(False)` pauses a graph its owner detached — `LiveSessionScreen._set_view` does it for the hidden Metrics/Raw view (a detached graph keeps its size, so after one visit to Raw the hidden ~5000-point raw graph redrew every tick at ~130 ms); data keeps accruing and the graph redraws once re-enabled. Measured on the phone 2026-10-01 (live metrics, ~26-min zoom): redraw max 160–200 → 38–48 ms and frame gap max 205–250 → 66–72 ms over the first ~5 min. (5) a zoomed-out redraw draws at most 4 points per pixel column (#70): `_fold_columns(values, stops)` keeps each pixel column's first, last, min and max sample (M4 aggregation, Jugel et al., VLDB 2014), in sample order — the line enters and leaves each column at its real samples and spans its real extremes, so it rasterizes like the full series (within 1 px: a thick stroke's half-width reaches the next column) and no peak or dip is lost. The columns are the viewport's pixel columns, computed once per redraw for all series by `_column_bounds(n, offset, per_px)` (a sample's column is `floor((offset + i) / per_px)`, the boundary estimate corrected by that same rule against float rounding), so the folded line moves exactly as the full one would; value labels, markers, the threshold and the time axis keep using the real indices. Folding starts where it is cheaper than drawing every point, which depends on what a point costs: `_FOLD_MIN_PER_PX_HEATMAP` = 4 samples per pixel for the heatmap line (two quads, a patch and texture coordinates per point), `_FOLD_MIN_PER_PX_LINE` = 8 for a plain line (desktop, 2026-10-02: a line at 4/px was 0.9 ms unfolded vs 1.2 folded, at 8/px 1.9 vs 1.4). Only C built-ins touch every sample (`min`/`max`/`list.index` on per-column slices). Desktop (2026-10-02): the live metrics graph with six series at a 3 h zoom 87 → 12 ms (Shamatha alone 39 → 4.4), the raw EEG graph zoomed out to its 60 s buffer 8.1 → 2.1 ms; the 5-minute view and the raw graph at its default 10 s (5 samples per pixel, cheaper unfolded) are unchanged. On the phone (2026-10-02, the same two steps before and after): the live Raw EEG view, zoomed out over most of its 60 s buffer (the live graphs' zoom is linked and saved), went from 48–89 ms average per 30 s window (max 175 ms) to 6–34 ms (max 49 ms) at 2 Hz — still growing with the buffer, since `min`/`max`/`list.index` touch every visible sample in C — and a long session's detail graph zoomed out from 52–68 ms max to ~19 ms (one 42 ms window). The unfolded path also got cheaper (the auto-scale copies each series once and takes its peak in C): desktop band graph, 8 series × 600 points, 1.46 → 1.16 ms. The heatmap Mesh splitter still matters: a wide fullscreen graph draws up to 4 points per pixel.

## Touch

- **Glyph hit-testing is done in WINDOW coordinates** (`_touch_in_window_rect`): the touch's window position (`self.to_window(*touch.pos)` — `touch.pos` is in the graph's parent frame; `touch.sx/sy` ignore Settings → Display rotation) against the glyph's rendered window rect (`self.to_window(...)` of `_expand_icon_rect`). The former "mid-chain frame that matches neither the canvas rect nor `to_widget`" (on-device click logging) was `GraphAwareScrollView` forwarding touches **untransformed**, still in its own parent's frame, while a graph's coordinates are content coordinates (#66); it now delivers them in the graph's parent frame (`_dispatch_to_graph`). The draw (`_draw_expand_icon`) and the hit-test use the **same** rect, so the visible box is exactly the tap target (no padding mismatch). `app/ui/touch_utils.point_in_rect` is the shared rect test. The History/user-picker manual routers happen to work with raw `collide_point` because their handlers are bound where the touch is already in the right frame — they're not under `GraphAwareScrollView`.
- **`GraphAwareScrollView._graph_under_touch` hit-tests in WINDOW coordinates** — `self.to_window(*touch.pos)` against each graph's rendered window rect (`_window_rect`, via `point_in_rect`), and only inside the viewport's own window rect — because a ScrollView translates its canvas, so a child's `x`/`y` are content coordinates: the old `graph.collide_point(*touch.pos)` (touch in the view's parent frame) let a graph scrolled out of view claim taps on whatever sat in its unscrolled rect — in the session detail scrolled down to Notes, every Notes tap went to the off-screen graph below it (#66). A touch on a graph goes straight to it (pan, pinch, tap, glyphs — no ScrollView hold-to-decide delay), in the graph's parent frame, except a **vertical swipe**: `ScrollableGraphWidget._hand_off_vertical_swipe` hands a single-finger touch that moved >20 dp mostly vertically, before any pan or pinch in the gesture, to **the view that routed it** (`touch.ud[GraphAwareScrollView.ROUTED_BY]`, set by `_dispatch_to_graph` — never a walk up the tree: Kivy's `Window.parent` is the Window itself, so a graph with no such ancestor, e.g. fullscreen, looped forever) via `GraphAwareScrollView.scroll_with`, which takes it only if the page can scroll, isn't already scrolling under another finger and the finger is still inside it (otherwise the graph keeps the touch); the graph releases the touch and undoes any pan only on success. The graph has no vertical gesture, and in landscape scroll mode the live graph fills the viewport the user scrolls to reach the stats and Start/Stop (the old wrong-frame routing left an accidental ~100 px scroll band that the fix would otherwise have taken away). `_grabbed_touches` is uid → that finger's `start`/`pos` in the graph's parent frame: pinch reads positions from it (never another touch's `.pos`, whose frame depends on where it was last dispatched), each finger is measured from its own start, and when a pinch ends the remaining finger pans on from where it is. A tap needs no movement on either axis (`_tap_cancelled`) — a vertical drag the graph keeps used to count as a tap and place a marker. `tests/test_graph_scroll_touch.py` uses the session detail's real shape (notes above, graph at the bottom) and the live body's (graph filling a translated viewport): taps across the visible graph, vertical swipe scrolls, horizontal drag pans, pinch never scrolls. **`GraphAwareScrollView.on_touch_down`/`on_scroll_start` only intercept touches inside the ScrollView's own viewport** (`self.collide_point` guard). A graph's *logical* bounds extend past the visible viewport when the body overflows (scroll mode), so `_graph_under_touch` would otherwise match — and steal — touches aimed at widgets sitting outside the scroll (e.g. the Metrics/Raw toggle above it). Regression-tested in `tests/test_ui_widgets.py::TestGraphAwareScrollViewGuard`.

## Fullscreen

- Fullscreen expand (`EEGMeditationApp._present_graph_fullscreen`, wired by `_wire_graph_expand`): **reparents** the graph (not a clone) into a full-window `FloatLayout` overlay on `self._float_root` with `size_hint=(1,1)` + `pos_hint={"x":0,"y":0}` so it reaches every edge — a `Popup` insets its content with chrome. Reparenting keeps the single `Window.on_mouse_down` bind and the live `add_point` feed, so a running session graph updates live in fullscreen. While fullscreen the expand glyph is the close cross (`closes=True`); it, Android back (`_fullscreen_close`) and Esc restore the graph to its original parent/index and give it the expand glyph back. **Capture `list(size_hint)`/`list(size)` copies before overriding** — they are live `ReferenceListProperty`s; a bare reference tracks the `(1,1)` you set and breaks restore.
